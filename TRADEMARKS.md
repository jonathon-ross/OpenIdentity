# OpenIdentity Trademark and Branding Guidance

The Apache License, Version 2.0 governs copyright and applicable patent rights for this repository. It does not grant general rights to OpenIdentity trade names, trademarks, service marks, certification marks, logos, or product names.

This document provides project branding guidance. It does not claim that every term described here is a registered trademark.

## Descriptive use

Reasonable, truthful descriptions may include “implements the OpenIdentity Protocol v0.1”, “compatible with the OpenIdentity protocol”, “built using OpenIdentity reference tooling”, “a fork of the OpenIdentity project”, or “based on OpenIdentity Protocol v0.1”. Such statements should not imply sponsorship, endorsement, certification, or official status when none exists.

## Modified implementations and forks

Forks and modified implementations should use branding that clearly distinguishes them from the official OpenIdentity project. If protocol behavior has changed, do not describe that modified protocol as an official OpenIdentity release.

## Official-status language

Unless expressly authorized by the OpenIdentity project, do not use branding or claims that reasonably imply official approval, such as “Official OpenIdentity”, “OpenIdentity Certified”, “OpenIdentity Certification”, or “OpenIdentity Approved”.

This does not prevent truthful statements that software passes publicly available conformance tests. Until an official certification program exists, “passes the OpenIdentity v0.1 public conformance tests” is preferable to “OpenIdentity Certified”.

## Logos

No license to an OpenIdentity logo or graphical brand asset is granted merely because repository source code or specifications are licensed under Apache-2.0. Official logo assets and rules may be published separately later.

## Product and company names

Third parties may use their own product/company names and accurately describe OpenIdentity compatibility. They should not use names that falsely suggest they are the official OpenIdentity project, governing organization, or an endorsed/certified OpenIdentity service.

## Canonical releases

Canonical OpenIdentity releases are identified by the official project repository together with their normative specifications, schemas, conformance vectors, release tags, and integrity artifacts. Permission to fork or modify repository materials does not by itself make a derivative protocol an official OpenIdentity release.

## Future certification

The project may later establish an official conformance/certification program and corresponding branding rules. Until then, passing public test vectors should be described factually and not represented as an official certification.
